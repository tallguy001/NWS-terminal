    Tropical weather outlooks

    From the National Hurricane Center: its GIS layers through NOAA's map service (storm positions, forecast cones and tracks, coastal watches and warnings, the outlook's areas), and the Tropical Weather Outlook text from the NWS API. The cone shows where the centre is likely to go, not the size of the storm: wind, rain and surge reach well outside it. Atlantic and eastern Pacific seasons run to November 30. For decisions, follow the hurricane center and your local NWS office.

    Model guidance

    GFS — global forecast system
    NOAA’s flagship global model. FV3 dynamical core, 13km inner nest (25km global), 4× daily cycling (00/06/12/18Z) with hybrid 4D-EnVar assimilation. Forecasts to 384h (16 days). Primary input to NBM and downstream models. Best for: medium-range planning, large-scale pattern recognition, tropical cyclone tracks.

    ECMWF IFS — integrated forecasting system
    European Centre’s operational physics-based global model. Spectral transform core at TCo1279 (~9km), 4D-Var assimilation cycling 4× daily. Consistently ranked the world’s most accurate medium-range NWP model. Forecasts to 360h (15 days). Best for: medium-range accuracy, European weather, tropical cyclones, jet-stream patterns.

    GEFS ensemble — 30 members
    NOAA Global Ensemble Forecast System. 30 perturbed members + control run, each starting from slightly different initial conditions. Wider spaghetti spread = more uncertainty. 0.25° global, 10-day range.

    Forecast verification — HRRR + RRFS vs URMA
    Compares what HRRR and RRFS forecast, a chosen number of hours ahead, with what URMA analysed for the same hour, over the past 24 hours. URMA is NOAA’s analysis of record — a higher-quality rerun of RTMA with late-arriving observations, issued about 7 hours after the hour it describes. MAE (mean absolute error) is the average size of the miss.

    Storm chances — SPC calibrated guidance
    Storm Prediction Center guidance built on the REFS ensemble: the chance of at least one cloud-to-ground lightning strike within about 12 miles of this location, for each hour, each 4-hour window and each outlook day, out to 60 hours. The table adds SPC’s calibrated chances of a tornado, severe hail and damaging wind. New runs four times a day. The same guidance is mapped under Forecast Maps › SPC guidance.
    Fetch a location to load thunderstorm guidance...
    Chance of a thunderstorm (%)
    Each point is the chance during the 1 hour, and during the 4 hours, ending at that time.
    By 4-hour window and outlook day
    Thunder is the thunderstorm chance for the 4 hours shown; Peak hour is the highest single hour inside them. An outlook day runs from 12 UTC to 12 UTC. Its chance is for the whole day, so it is at least as high as any window in it.
    Source: GribStream.com — SPC REFS calibrated thunderstorm guidance and SPC GREFS calibrated severe-weather guidance (NOAA Storm Prediction Center). Guidance, not a forecast or a warning.
    Snow and ice odds — National Blend of Models
    For this location, hour by hour for about two days and then in 6-hour blocks out to 7 days: the snowfall the blend expects, the range it is likely to fall in, the chance of passing set amounts, the chance of each precipitation type, and ice. Days 1 to 3 are mapped under Forecast Maps › Winter.
    Fetch a location to load snow and ice odds...
    By hour, then by 6-hour block
    Expected is the blend's single best amount for the hour or block, in inches. Range runs from the 10th to the 90th percentile: one time in ten it comes in under the low figure and one time in ten over the high one. The columns headed 0.1 in+ to 4 in+ are the percent chance of at least that much snow within that one hour or block; they cannot be added across rows. Ice is the expected freezing-rain ice in inches. Type is the percent chance that each kind of precipitation is falling at the end of the hour or block, likeliest first.
    Source: GribStream.com — NOAA National Blend of Models, probabilistic snow, ice and precipitation-type fields. Guidance, not the official forecast.
    Forecast trend — how the blend has changed
    The National Blend of Models forecast for this location as it stands now and as it stood 12, 24 and 48 hours ago, side by side: temperature every three hours for five days, and rain and snow totals for the next three days. A forecast that keeps moving the same way is still settling; one that holds steady has earned more trust.
    Fetch a location to load the forecast trend...
    Temperature, each version of the forecast (°F)
    The thick line is the current forecast. Where the older lines sit above or below it, the forecast has since cooled or warmed.
    Warmest and coldest of each day, by version
    Warmest and coldest are the highest and lowest of the three-hourly temperatures in each calendar day, so they can sit a degree or two inside the true high and low. Every version is sampled at the same times. −12 h, −24 h and −48 h are the forecast as it stood that long ago.
    Source: GribStream.com — NOAA National Blend of Models, current run and the runs available 12, 24 and 48 hours ago.
    RAP — rapid refresh (13km, hourly)
    NOAA’s hourly-cycling mesoscale model for North America. 13km grid, hybrid ensemble-3DVar assimilation ingesting radar, satellite, aircraft & surface obs every hour. Key input to NBM. Forecasts to 51h. Best for: aviation, convective initiation, frontal passages, renewable energy ramps.

    RRFS — rapid refresh forecast system (3km)
    NOAA’s next-gen storm-scale model replacing HRRR & RAP. FV3 dynamical core, 3km convection-allowing, hourly cycling with radar/satellite/aircraft assimilation. Unified North America domain. Forecasts to 84h. Experimental — 15-day archive, may change without notice. Best for: severe storms, lightning, hail, updraft helicity, aviation ceilings.

    AIFS — ECMWF AI forecast system
    ECMWF’s machine-learning weather model, trained on ERA5 reanalysis. Runs on ECMWF’s GPU cluster at 0.25° resolution (25km). Produces 15-day forecasts in seconds vs hours for IFS. Uses graph neural networks to learn atmospheric dynamics directly from data. Competitive with IFS on many metrics, especially in the tropics and for medium-range.

    NOAA’s operational AI Global Forecast System, built on Google DeepMind’s GraphCast. It replaced the experimental GraphCastGFS feed, which stopped on May 5, 2026. Four runs a day, 6-hour steps to 16 days, 0.25° grid. Temperature and wind are shown here.
